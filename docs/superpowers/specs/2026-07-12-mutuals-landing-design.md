# Mutuals Landing Page Design

## Purpose

Build a focused conversion page for founders and senior startup operators with a real product, actual customers, or clear intent and authority to act. The page should make Mutuals feel like a high-agency company representative rather than a directory, founder community, lead database, or passive recommender.

The approved visual target is [`design/reference/mutuals-objective-field.png`](../../../design/reference/mutuals-objective-field.png).

## Primary promise

Use this hero copy verbatim:

> Mutuals represents a company’s interests, discovers non-obvious revenue paths, initiates the right conversations, and advances them until a human decision is required.

Most of the sentence uses soft charcoal. The final clause, “a human decision is required,” receives near-black emphasis. The graph at the right visualizes movement from signal to decision.

Primary action: **Represent my company**.

Secondary action: **See how an opportunity moves**.

## Audience

The ideal visitor is a founder or empowered operator whose startup has at least one of:

- a working product;
- actual customers or users;
- a credible offer and the intent to execute quickly.

The site should repel passive networking, spray-and-pray lead generation, and vague partnership requests. It should attract people willing to share context, respond quickly, and authorize Mutuals to act.

## Visual system

- Warm toned white, not stark white.
- Soft charcoal for most copy; true near-black only for decisive text and controls.
- Large negative space and a strict grid.
- Hairline coordinate fields that imply objective, active work.
- A single right-side opportunity graph in the hero: Signal captured → Conversation initiated → Interest aligned → Human decision required.
- Editorial scale without decorative excess.
- No gradients, shadows, stock imagery, logo walls, testimonials, pricing, feature grids, or generic AI visuals.
- Every visible element must explain the promise, show the method, qualify the visitor, or enable application.

## Information architecture

The top bar contains four anchored links:

1. How it works
2. For startups
3. About
4. Apply

The header remains visually quiet. On small screens, the four links remain accessible through a compact disclosure menu.

### Hero

- Exact primary promise.
- Primary and secondary actions.
- Right-side opportunity graph.
- One proof-of-discipline line: “One company at a time. One opportunity until it moves.”

### How it works

Show five case-study cards in a single row at desktop width. The row becomes a purposeful horizontal rail on smaller screens rather than wrapping into a generic grid.

Because Mutuals does not yet have five verified public outcomes, each item is explicitly marked **Illustrative case study**. This avoids inventing traction while demonstrating the full operating method.

Each card contains only:

- opportunity type;
- a specific one-line thesis;
- the value-path equation in compressed form;
- a clear “Open case study” affordance.

Opening a card reveals an accessible full-screen detail dialog. Each study contains:

1. Situation
2. Signal captured
3. Hidden leverage
4. Proposed counterpart
5. Value exchanged by each side
6. Smallest two-sided test
7. Mutuals’ first approach
8. 30-day execution sequence
9. Success measures
10. Risks and trust constraints
11. Human decision gate

The five illustrative studies should cover distinct value-path archetypes:

- support automation × product analytics;
- wellness commerce × local service touchpoint;
- spend management × compliance expertise;
- developer tooling × implementation channel;
- mobility demand × hospitality distribution.

Canonical study content is fixed below so implementation does not invent claims.

#### Study 01 — Resolve the moment of friction

- **Opportunity type:** Support automation × product analytics
- **Thesis:** When analytics detects a customer stuck inside a repeatable workflow, an action-taking support agent can complete that workflow before frustration becomes a ticket or churn signal.
- **Equation:** An unresolved in-product task × a browser-action agent × a detected friction moment × rising support cost → one bounded assisted completion.
- **Situation:** A SaaS company sees repeat tickets around a multi-step account workflow. Its analytics platform can detect rage clicks and repeated failed attempts, but can only report the problem.
- **Signal captured:** The same workflow produces a concentrated cluster of rage clicks, repeat visits, and support requests.
- **Hidden leverage:** The analytics product knows exactly when help is needed; the support agent can act in the browser rather than return instructions.
- **Proposed counterpart:** A product-analytics or digital-adoption startup with live friction signals and customers asking what to do after detection.
- **Value exchange:** The analytics partner gains a measurable remediation layer and stronger outcome story. The support agent gains qualified design customers, real procedures, and production learning.
- **Smallest two-sided test:** One consenting customer cohort, one reversible workflow, and one 30-day pilot with explicit action limits.
- **First approach:** “You already identify the point of failure. We represent a team that can safely complete one bounded workflow at that exact moment. Would you test whether detection plus action improves completion for one customer cohort?”
- **30-day sequence:** Verify baseline and permissions; integrate one signal and workflow; run a supervised cohort; review completion, escalation, and incident evidence.
- **Success measures:** Workflow completion; avoided tickets; time to resolution; human-escalation rate; incorrect-action incidents.
- **Risks:** Excessive permissions; actions in ambiguous states; user consent; cross-product data handling; confusing assistance with autonomous authority.
- **Human decision gate:** Both founders approve the permitted actions, customer cohort, escalation policy, and continuation only after baseline evidence is credible.

#### Study 02 — Continue a customer’s intention

- **Opportunity type:** Wellness commerce × local service touchpoint
- **Thesis:** A wellness purchase reveals a moment of intent that a trusted local service can continue without turning the brand’s post-purchase experience into an advertisement.
- **Equation:** A completed wellness purchase × available local-service capacity × the post-purchase moment × a newly opened location → one permissioned local invitation.
- **Situation:** A wellness brand completes the sale online. A nearby spa has unused weekday capacity and needs qualified local discovery.
- **Signal captured:** Customers in selected postcodes buy a product associated with an ongoing routine rather than a one-time transaction.
- **Hidden leverage:** The brand owns a high-trust post-purchase touchpoint; the spa can turn the product promise into a physical experience.
- **Proposed counterpart:** A quality-controlled spa or wellness studio with a newly opened location and transparent service standards.
- **Value exchange:** The brand adds useful continuity and learns about offline engagement. The spa receives qualified local demand. Customers receive a relevant option rather than a generic promotion.
- **Smallest two-sided test:** A non-medical, opt-in invitation to 100 eligible purchasers in two postcodes, with no customer data shared before consent.
- **First approach:** “Your new location can be useful at the precise moment these customers begin a wellness routine. Would you reserve a small, trackable experience for an opt-in cohort rather than run a broad discount?”
- **30-day sequence:** Approve brand and service fit; define consent and offer; run the cohort; compare redemption, repeat behavior, and complaints.
- **Success measures:** Opt-in rate; qualified booking rate; redemption; repeat purchase or visit; opt-outs and complaints.
- **Risks:** Health claims; inappropriate location use; discount-led demand; inconsistent service quality; brand-safety mismatch.
- **Human decision gate:** Both companies approve the message, service standard, economics, customer-consent flow, and any public association.

#### Study 03 — Remove the next operational blocker

- **Opportunity type:** Spend management × compliance expertise
- **Thesis:** A spend platform can identify when a growing customer is blocked by vendor approval or certification and route help before the operational delay becomes churn.
- **Equation:** A blocked vendor workflow × trusted compliance expertise × the approval moment × an approaching deadline → one scoped compliance intervention.
- **Situation:** Customers use a spend tool successfully but repeatedly stall when security reviews, vendor onboarding, or certification work begins.
- **Signal captured:** Support and product data show repeated questions immediately before a vendor is approved or a larger contract can proceed.
- **Hidden leverage:** The spend platform sees the operational moment and has customer trust; the compliance partner has expertise but usually arrives too late.
- **Proposed counterpart:** A compliance platform or specialist advisor with a bounded onboarding offer and clear professional limits.
- **Value exchange:** The spend product reduces a customer blocker and improves retention. The compliance partner receives high-intent work. The customer moves an existing purchase or contract forward.
- **Smallest two-sided test:** Ten opted-in customers with the same approval blocker receive a fixed-scope diagnostic and next-step plan.
- **First approach:** “We can identify ten customers already delayed by the same approval problem. Can you offer one bounded diagnostic with transparent scope, price, and handoff so we can measure whether it shortens approval time?”
- **30-day sequence:** Classify the repeated blocker; approve disclosures and scope; run ten diagnostics; compare approval time and support load.
- **Success measures:** Time to approved vendor; diagnostic-to-engagement rate; support tickets avoided; customer satisfaction; disclosed referral economics.
- **Risks:** Regulated advice; opaque referral fees; conflicted recommendations; sensitive company documents; poorly bounded delivery.
- **Human decision gate:** The client approves referral disclosure, professional boundaries, data access, commercial terms, and the list of eligible customers.

#### Study 04 — Complete the product with delivery

- **Opportunity type:** Developer tooling × implementation channel
- **Thesis:** When a technically strong tool loses qualified trials during setup, a trusted implementation partner can turn product capability into customer time-to-value.
- **Equation:** A stalled qualified trial × implementation capacity × the setup moment × buyer urgency → one fixed-scope activation sprint.
- **Situation:** A developer product earns serious trials, but a portion stall during integration because the buyer lacks time or specialist capacity.
- **Signal captured:** Qualified accounts repeatedly reach the same setup step, open documentation, contact support, and then go inactive.
- **Hidden leverage:** The product company can identify high-intent stalled accounts; a consultancy can deliver the missing implementation and gain a differentiated offer.
- **Proposed counterpart:** A focused cloud or software consultancy with referenceable delivery quality and capacity for a fixed-scope sprint.
- **Value exchange:** The product converts more qualified trials and reduces founder-led onboarding. The consultancy receives scoped work and a repeatable specialization. Customers reach value sooner.
- **Smallest two-sided test:** Three qualified stalled accounts are offered an optional, fixed-deliverable activation sprint with separate consent and pricing.
- **First approach:** “Three qualified teams are blocked at the same integration step. Would you define a fixed-scope sprint that gets them to the first successful production outcome without taking over the product relationship?”
- **30-day sequence:** Select the repeated setup boundary; agree ownership and quality bar; deliver three sprints; review activation, margin, and customer feedback.
- **Success measures:** Trial-to-activation rate; time to first production result; support hours saved; sprint margin; customer ownership disputes.
- **Risks:** Unclear account ownership; inconsistent delivery; support liability; consultancy dependence; services masking product flaws.
- **Human decision gate:** Both companies approve the statement of work, customer ownership, support boundaries, quality standard, and pricing.

#### Study 05 — Use a physical moment already in motion

- **Opportunity type:** Mobility demand × hospitality distribution
- **Thesis:** A hospitality company and a mobility product can improve the same arrival or departure moment by coordinating a useful guest handoff rather than buying generic reach.
- **Equation:** A guest arrival need × reliable mobility capacity × the booking or checkout moment × predictable route demand → one coordinated transfer flow.
- **Situation:** Boutique hotels repeatedly help guests arrange transport. A mobility startup needs concentrated, repeatable demand on valuable routes.
- **Signal captured:** Front desks handle the same transfer questions while the mobility company sees recurring pickup and return patterns around hotel clusters.
- **Hidden leverage:** Hotels own trusted pre-arrival and checkout touchpoints; the mobility company owns fulfillment and route intelligence.
- **Proposed counterpart:** A small hotel group with high guest-service standards and enough concentrated arrival volume for a controlled pilot.
- **Value exchange:** Hotels reduce staff coordination and improve guest experience. The mobility company receives qualified demand and repeat routes. Guests receive a clearer, accountable option.
- **Smallest two-sided test:** Two hotels run a four-week opt-in transfer flow for one airport route with no exclusivity.
- **First approach:** “Your teams already coordinate this route manually. Would you test one accountable transfer flow at booking and checkout, with service failures visible to both sides and no exclusivity?”
- **30-day sequence:** Map the repeated route and failure modes; agree service and escalation standards; launch at two properties; review trips, incidents, and front-desk effort.
- **Success measures:** Booking-to-trip conversion; completed trips; service incidents; guest satisfaction; front-desk minutes saved; repeat-route rate.
- **Risks:** Service failures reflecting on the hotel; inappropriate data sharing; accessibility needs; exclusivity pressure; demand spikes.
- **Human decision gate:** Both operators approve the service-level agreement, brand placement, data boundaries, escalation process, and continuation threshold.

### For startups

Label: **For startups**.

Headline:

> A startup begins as a small living idea. Then execution asks it to become a company.

Supporting thought: Mutuals learns the startup’s present reality—its product, customers, constraints, leverage, and immediate need—then finds and advances the external relationships that can support the next step toward its vision.

The section should feel empathetic without becoming sentimental or infantilizing.

### About

Headline:

> Competition explains how value is divided. We look for value that has not been created yet.

Explain concisely that mainstream startup and business narratives map competitors while Mutuals maps complementary assets, needs, timing, trust, and customer touchpoints. Mutuals does not force companies together; it searches for natural, two-sided value and carries the first action.

### Apply

The application asks only for:

- company URL;
- work email;
- phone number.

All fields are required and validated. Keep the page decisive: “No deck required. We start with the company itself.”

Validation and normalization contracts:

- **Company URL:** trim whitespace; accept `http://` or `https://` URLs and bare public domains containing a dot; normalize a bare domain to `https://`; reject other schemes, spaces, single-label hosts, localhost, and private IP addresses. Valid: `argide.ai`, `https://www.argide.ai/demo`. Invalid: `argide`, `ftp://argide.ai`, `not a url`.
- **Work email:** trim and lowercase; require one `@`, a non-empty local part, and a domain containing a dot. Request a work address but do not maintain a free-email blacklist because a zero-to-one founder may legitimately operate from one. Valid: `founder@argide.ai`, `founder+mutuals@gmail.com`. Invalid: `founder`, `@argide.ai`, `founder@localhost`.
- **Phone:** trim; accept an optional leading `+` plus spaces, parentheses, periods, and hyphens; normalize to an optional leading `+` followed by digits; require 8–15 digits and do not infer a country. Valid: `+91 98765 43210`, `(415) 555-0132`. Invalid: `12345`, `call-me`, a number over 15 digits.
- Empty fields use specific required messages. Non-empty invalid fields use format-specific messages. Errors are associated with fields and announced through an accessible summary.

The local feedback build has no submission backend. Submitting shows a polished success state that clearly says the prototype did not transmit the information. The submission boundary stays isolated so a real endpoint can be connected later without changing the interface.

## Interaction requirements

- Anchored navigation scrolls to sections and accounts for the sticky header.
- Primary and secondary hero actions land on Apply and How it works.
- Every case-study card is keyboard accessible.
- The detail dialog traps focus, closes with Escape or its explicit back control, restores focus to the originating card, and prevents background scrolling.
- Form errors are field-specific and announced accessibly.
- The prototype success state can be reset for repeated review.
- Motion is limited to subtle graph/path and affordance transitions and respects reduced-motion preferences.

## Responsive behavior

- Desktop: hero splits copy and graph; five cards occupy one horizontal row.
- Tablet: hero remains two-column where legible; case studies become a horizontal rail.
- Mobile: copy precedes the graph; nav condenses; case-study rail retains snap points; dialog becomes a full-screen reading surface; form fields stack.

## Success criteria

- The first viewport communicates the exact promise without requiring scrolling.
- Visitors can distinguish Mutuals from a lead-gen agency or founder club.
- The five case studies explain the actual operating logic without fabricated results.
- The founder metaphor communicates care and execution, not immaturity.
- The application completes with three fields and honest prototype feedback.
- The built page visually retains the approved target’s negative space, graph-right composition, warm palette, typographic hierarchy, and objective character.
